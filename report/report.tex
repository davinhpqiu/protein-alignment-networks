\section*{Abstract}
Sequence-similarity networks encode protein sequences as vertices and selected
pairwise similarities as edges. Resulting topology depends on protein sample,
score method, and edge rule. We implemented a reproducible pipeline and tested
these choices on \num{\MasterDomains} Pfam domains from \MasterFamilies\ families
and \MasterClans\ clans. A factorial design generated
\num{\SampledCollections} balanced collections and
\num{\GraphsEvaluated} weighted graphs using exact local alignment or BLAST,
three edge-rule families, and Louvain community detection. Adjusted mutual
information (AMI) measured recovery of withheld Pfam labels. At matched 2\%
edge density, median absolute family-NMI difference between methods was
\MatchedTwoAbsDeltaNMI. Equal score percentiles produced unequal densities.
Score permutation reduced reference-setting family AMI from
\ObservedTopFiveAMI\ to \NullTopFiveAMI. Results quantify sampling and edge
selection effects in protein-network analysis.

\section{Introduction}

A protein sequence is a finite word over 20 amino acids. Pairwise alignment
assigns similarity from residue substitutions and gaps. Smith--Waterman gives
an exact local optimum \citep{smith1981identification}; Gotoh's recurrence
supports affine gap cost \citep{gotoh1982improved}. BLOSUM matrices supply
empirical substitution scores \citep{henikoff1992amino}. BLAST uses seed-based
search to accelerate local comparison \citep{altschul1990basic,camacho2009blastplus}.
DEDAL learns sequence embeddings and alignment parameters
\citep{llinareslopez2023deep}.

An all-pairs score table can define a sequence-similarity network (SSN).
SSNs support protein-family exploration and clustering
\citep{atkinson2009sequence,gerlt2015efi,enright2002mcl}. Edge rules, missing
scores, and collection composition alter topology
\citep{apeltsin2011threshold,hornung2023objective}.

\textbf{Research question.} How do graphs built from different protein
collections, alignment algorithms, and graph-construction rules compare?

Work comprises a tested protein-to-graph pipeline, hierarchical Pfam sampling,
and repeated evaluation against withheld family and clan labels.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Master panel}

Pfam defines families of homologous domains using curated seed alignments and
profile hidden Markov models; clans group families with evidence of common
ancestry \citep{mistry2021pfam}. Graph vertices represent seed-domain instances.

Panel selection used recorded seed \SamplingSeed. Candidate clans came from Pfam's
official family--clan catalogue and required at least three member families.
Clan order and within-clan family order were independently shuffled. For each
candidate family, InterPro metadata had to report type \emph{domain} or
\emph{family} and at least \TargetPerFamily\ seed domains. First \MasterClans\
clans containing three
eligible families were retained. Audit tables store each inspected acceptance
and rejection.

Each selected seed alignment was filtered to unique sequences containing only
20 standard amino acids and consistent domain coordinates.
\TargetPerFamily\ representatives
per family were selected by deterministic farthest-point sampling. Initial
representative had length nearest family median. Each subsequent representative
maximised minimum aligned distance to selected set, where distance equals one
minus residue identity over jointly occupied alignment positions. Ties used node
identifier order. Alignment gaps were removed before scoring. Panel contains
\num{\MasterDomains} domains, \MasterFamilies\ families, \MasterClans\ clans, and
\(\binom{\MasterDomains}{2}=\num{\MasterPairs}\) unordered pairs.

\subsection{Repeated collections}

For one condition \((c,f,r)\), \(c\) clans were sampled uniformly without
replacement; \(f\) families were then sampled within each clan; \(r\) domains
were sampled within each family. Every collection therefore contained \(cfr\)
vertices and balanced family sizes. Design crossed
\[
c\in\{2,4,8\},\quad f\in\{2,3\},\quad r\in\{10,20,40\}.
\]
\ReplicatesPerCondition\ seeded replicates for each of
\(3\cdot2\cdot3=\ConditionCount\) conditions produced
\(\ConditionCount\cdot\ReplicatesPerCondition=\SampledCollections\)
collections. A stable hash of base seed and condition generated each replicate
seed. Every score method and graph rule used identical stored membership for a
given collection.

Sampling without replacement can yield duplicate complete node sets across
replicates when a condition exhausts available choices. Four composition
conditions contained duplicates. Their \ExcludedCollections\ collections remain
in audit tables; distributional summaries use \PrimaryCollections\ collections
from \PrimaryConditions\ unique-set conditions.

\subsection{Pairwise scores}

Let \(x=x_1\ldots x_p\) and \(y=y_1\ldots y_q\). Define substitution score
\(a(x_i,y_j)\), gap-open cost \(g_o>0\), and gap-extension cost \(g_e>0\).
Affine-gap Smith--Waterman matrices satisfy
\begin{align*}
E_{ij}&=\max\{H_{i-1,j}-g_o,\ E_{i-1,j}-g_e\},\\
F_{ij}&=\max\{H_{i,j-1}-g_o,\ F_{i,j-1}-g_e\},\\
H_{ij}&=\max\{0,H_{i-1,j-1}+a(x_i,y_j),E_{ij},F_{ij}\},
\end{align*}
with \(H_{i0}=H_{0j}=0\) and unavailable gap states \(-\infty\). A gap of
length \(\ell\) costs \(g_o+(\ell-1)g_e\). Local-alignment score is
\(s(x,y)=\max_{i,j}H_{ij}\). Biopython's \texttt{PairwiseAligner}
\citep{cock2009biopython} used local mode, BLOSUM62,
\(g_o=11\), and \(g_e=1\).

Local BLASTP used BLOSUM62, gap costs 11 and 1, composition-based statistics,
and reporting threshold \(E=10\). For raw score \(S\), graph weight was bit score
\[
S'=\frac{\lambda S-\log K}{\log 2},
\]
where \(\lambda\) and \(K\) depend on scoring system and background residue
frequencies \citep{karlin1990methods}. Highest-bit-score high-scoring segment
pair represented each ordered query--subject pair. Highest score across reported
directions represented each unordered pair. Unreported pairs remained missing.
BLAST reported \num{\BlastReportedPairs} of \num{\MasterPairs} master pairs
(\BlastCoveragePct\%). Pretrained DEDAL was checked on pilot data; scaled run was
excluded after measured runtime exceeded available compute budget.

\subsection{Graph construction}

For a collection of \(n\) vertices, let
\(V=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), \(P=\binom n2\), and \(A_m\subseteq\binom V2\) be
pairs reported by score method \(m\). Weighted undirected graph
\(G_{m,\theta}=(V,E_{m,\theta},w_m)\) uses
\(w_m(\{i,j\})=s^m_{ij}\). Three rule families define edge set:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Percentile \(p\).} If \(q_p^m\) is empirical \(p\)-quantile of
  \(\{s^m_{ij}:\{i,j\}\in A_m\}\), retain
  \(E=\{\{i,j\}\in A_m:s^m_{ij}\ge q_p^m\}\).
  \item \textbf{Target density \(d\).} Rank \(A_m\) by decreasing score and
  retain first \(\min\{|A_m|,\lceil dP\rceil\}\) pairs. Realised density is
  \(|E|/P\). A shortfall records \(\lceil dP\rceil-|E|>0\).
  \item \textbf{Top-\(k\).} For each \(i\), retain its \(k\) highest-scoring
  available incident pairs. Undirected edge set is union across vertices,
  following local-neighbour network construction \citep{camoglu2006multiattribute}.
\end{enumerate}
Parameters were
\begin{align*}
p&\in\{0.90,0.95,0.98,0.99\},\\
d&\in\{0.005,0.01,0.02,0.05\},\\
k&\in\{1,2,5,10\}.
\end{align*}
Two score methods and 12 rule settings generated
\(900\cdot2\cdot12=\num{\GraphsEvaluated}\) graphs. Each collection supplied
identical vertices to all 24 method--rule combinations.

\subsection{Communities and agreement}

Weighted Louvain optimisation \citep{blondel2008fast}, implemented in NetworkX
\citep{hagberg2008exploring}, maximised modularity
\[
Q=\frac{1}{2W}\sum_{i,j}\left(w_{ij}-\gamma\frac{k_i k_j}{2W}\right)
\mathbf 1\{u_i=u_j\}.
\]
Here \(w_{ij}\) is edge weight, \(k_i=\sum_j w_{ij}\) is weighted degree,
\(W=\frac12\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}\) is total edge weight, \(\gamma=1\) is resolution,
\(u_i\) is detected community, and \(\mathbf1\) is indicator function.

Let \(U\) denote detected partition and \(V^*\) Pfam reference partition.
For joint proportions \(p_{uv}\) and margins \(p_u,p_v\), define
\[
I(U;V^*)=\sum_{u,v}p_{uv}\log\frac{p_{uv}}{p_u p_v},\qquad
H(U)=-\sum_u p_u\log p_u.
\]
Arithmetic-normalised mutual information and adjusted mutual information are
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{NMI}(U,V^*)&=\frac{I(U;V^*)}{[H(U)+H(V^*)]/2},\\
\operatorname{AMI}(U,V^*)&=
\frac{I(U;V^*)-\mathbb E[I(U;V^*)]}
{[H(U)+H(V^*)]/2-\mathbb E[I(U;V^*)]}.
\end{align*}
Expectation uses random partitions with observed group sizes fixed. AMI controls
chance agreement caused by group count and fragmentation
\citep{vinh2010information}. Both metrics used scikit-learn
\citep{pedregosa2011scikitlearn}. Family and clan labels entered evaluation
after community detection.

\subsection{AMI corrected fragmentation-driven agreement}

NMI and AMI evaluate same detected partitions; metric choice changes reported
agreement. AMI subtracts expected mutual information under random partitions
with observed group-size margins. For BLAST percentile 0.99, median detected
community count was \BlastPercentileNinetyNineCommunities\ among 160 vertices,
while family reference contained 8 groups. Median family NMI was
\BlastPercentileNinetyNineNMI; corresponding AMI was
\BlastPercentileNinetyNineAMI. Chance correction therefore moves highly
fragmented settings towards zero while leaving dense, well-resolved settings
near their NMI values.

Figure~\ref{fig:ami-frequency} replots same graphs and bins by AMI. Low-density
percentile and target-density curves shift left; top-5 and top-10 curves remain
near high agreement. AMI is used for cross-rule and cross-composition recovery
claims because community count varies across those comparisons. NMI remains a
bounded secondary descriptor for paired settings with comparable
fragmentation.

\subsection{Sensitivity checks}

Within-collection score permutation preserved score distribution and rule
parameters while randomising pair identities. In reference condition
\((c,f,r)=(4,2,20)\), Biopython top-5 median family AMI changed from
\ObservedTopFiveAMI\ to \NullTopFiveAMI\ (Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}, left).
Positive null NMI confirmed finite-partition chance bias addressed by AMI\@.

Unweighted Louvain replaced every retained edge weight by one. Reference
Biopython top-5 median family AMI was \UnweightedTopFiveAMI, compared with
\ObservedTopFiveAMI\ under native weights (Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}, right).
For this setting, selected adjacency retained family-label agreement after edge
weights were removed.

\section{Discussion}

At 2\% matched density, median absolute family-NMI difference between
Biopython and BLAST was \MatchedTwoAbsDeltaNMI. Equal percentiles generated
different densities because BLAST supplied \BlastCoveragePct\% of master pairs.
Percentile comparisons therefore combine method, coverage, and edge-budget
effects.

At \(k=5\), median family AMI was \BioTopFiveAMI\ for Biopython and
\BlastTopFiveAMI\ for BLAST\@. Each vertex selects up to \(k\) available
neighbours before undirected union. Resulting global density depends on score
availability and neighbour overlap. Target density supplies a score-method
comparison with fixed edge budget.

NMI exceeded AMI under sparse, fragmented settings
(Figs.~\ref{fig:nmi-frequency} and~\ref{fig:ami-frequency}). AMI subtracts
expected mutual information conditional on observed group sizes, so cross-rule
and cross-composition interpretations use AMI\@. Paired fixed-composition method
comparison retains NMI as a secondary bounded descriptor.

Permutation results show that pair identity contains family information beyond
score distribution. Under unit weights, reference-setting family AMI was
\UnweightedTopFiveAMI, compared with \ObservedTopFiveAMI\ under native weights.
Controls locate observed signal in edge placement. Inference scope remains
sampled Pfam panel.

Panel design selected sequence-diverse Pfam seed domains and leaves natural
family abundance unestimated. Findings apply to balanced mixtures from
\MasterFamilies\
selected families. Full-length proteins, alternative databases, synthetic evolutionary
data, and structural labels require separate evaluation. \ExcludedCells\ saturated design
conditions reduced primary factorial design to \PrimaryConditions\ conditions.
Louvain resolution
and stochasticity received limited study. DEDAL lacks scaled evidence here.

Future work should repeat sampling across broader Pfam panels, model composition
effects with hierarchical uncertainty, test graph filtrations across continuous
edge budgets, and compare community algorithms. These experiments estimate
stability across panels and graph-construction choices.

\section{Conclusion}

Pipeline maps recorded domain collections to pair scores, weighted graphs,
communities, and auditable agreement measures. Matched-budget Biopython--BLAST
differences were smaller than equal-percentile differences. At \(k=5\), median
family AMI was \BioTopFiveAMI\ and \BlastTopFiveAMI, respectively. Target
density supplies controlled method comparison. Sampling frame, missing-score
policy, edge rule, and realised density should accompany every reported SSN
result.
